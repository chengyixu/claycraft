# Claycraft

Make the requested subject look small enough to hold and shaped by hand. For an image request, render and deliver the asset with the available image-generation tool. For an editable model request, build the same forms in the requested 3D format. Keep the brief’s subject, colors, dimensions and background treatment.

Open the closest bundled image before rendering. Use it for surface, lighting and scale. Let the brief supply the characters and props.

## Shape the asset

Start with the few pieces a sculptor would make. For a watering can, pinch a squat hollow body, roll a thick coil for the handle and taper the spout. Flatten the contact patches where pieces meet. Leave a slight lean, uneven rim or shallow tool mark where the hand would actually touch. Keep outlines legible at the final size.

Use dry, unglazed clay with fine irregular pores, softly worn edges and broad diffuse highlights. Vary surface marks with the object: score short grooves into a clay plank, press spots into a blanket, overlap flattened leaf shapes. Keep the grain fine enough that it recedes at thumbnail size. For the reference palette, start with oatmeal, sand and cocoa, then add a little dusty blue, sage or brick red. Retain color differences under warm light.

For figures, use an oversized rounded head, a compact body, stubby feet and two dark vertical eye impressions. A closed eyelid can be one curved groove. Build distinctive features from applied clay pieces. For objects, preserve the real openings and negative spaces that identify the subject.

## Frame and finish

For a standalone asset, show the whole silhouette with breathing room. For a set, reuse the first finished asset as a visual anchor and hold camera height, lighting direction, scale and grain constant. Create separate files when the brief calls for individual assets. Add a slab or scenery only when it belongs in the composition.

Light from a broad window or lamp to one side. Let shadows collect under feet and at joins. In a scene, soften the distant room or tabletop while keeping the focal action sharp. In a lineup, keep every face in focus.

Inspect the exported image at its intended size. If it reads as molded plastic, broaden the highlights and break the uniformity of a few edges. If the surface reads as sand or felt, reduce the bump and preserve smooth compressed areas. For transparent output, inspect the alpha channel and composite over light and dark backgrounds to catch halos or a baked-in checkerboard.
